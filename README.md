# Eclipse
## An alternative keyboard layout designed for typing on standard row-staggered keyboards
![eclipse](https://i.ibb.co/zWbbgn6H/A2900669-E6-CC-4-B40-A392-D17-B2-FB7836-A.jpg)


### Summary:

The main objective of Eclipse is to optimize for extremely low SFB percentage, especially on the left ring and pinky fingers because they lack dexterity and anatomical freedom advantages that stronger fingers like middle and index take advantage of with alt-fingering to achieve even lower SFB. The intuitive alt-fingering effectively halves the amount of SFB, which is a rare case where the SFB and alt-fingering are actually very close in percentage around 0.18%, which means it's easy to learn the fingerings and provides great bang for your buck usefulness in reducing SFB without requiring much extra effort.

ZXQJ are the lowest frequency letters so they are placed in the farthest corners of the keyboard to help reduce movement, stretches, and limits pinky/ring fingers to only 2 main keys rather than full 3 key columns, as well as limiting both indexes to 5 main keys rather than full 6 key index setups. 

The redirects and rolls are both on the low side, which isn't positive or negative. The alternations on the other hand are notably high which isn't necessarily bad either but many typists find rolls to be superior to alternation. The weakest aspect of Eclipse is its high SFS percentage, which is mostly caused by the left middle and left index finger columns, however this is at least slightly mitigated by being on dominant fingers. 


## Fingermap Analysis
![fingermap](https://i.ibb.co/XZfjN8yn/Screenshot-2026-11-12-at-3-19-33-AM.png)
### Left Pinky:
BNZ column is a popular pinky setup among modern layouts due to its low usage and good SFB/SFS which are great qualities for a pinky column as it is generally the weakest and least dextrous finger. SFBs in words like RAINBOW, BRONZE and SFS in BAND, ZONE are rare enough to make sure this the lowest SFB/SFS column in the layout.

### Left Ring:
FSX column is similar to BNZ since it's also popular as a pinky column found in other layouts, making it a great candidate for the ring finger to handle comfortably. Slightly higher SFS in this column in words like FAST SIX and FIX but using left middle for X can sometimes help reduce SFS and improve scissors in words like ANXIETY. 

### Left Middle:
RLH is one of the main sources of SFB and SFS but there is a plan. The letters are arranged in a triangular cluster rather than a straight column which is notable because it promotes lower movement in a small concentrated area as a result of all 3 keys being adjacent to one another. R is a particularly difficult letter to pair with other letters SFB wise, therefore it is placed on a strong and flexible middle finger allowing you to alt-finger almost all SFBs present like RL HR and HL in words like WORLD THROUGH and HIGHLY.

### Right Index:
GCWPYJ is a much lighter index and the main SFBs PY CY GY in words like TYPE FANCY and ENERGY are able to be alt-fingered away due to the central Y positioning. Because the SFBs here are solved with alt-fingering the bigger concern is the SFS but luckily the main sources like W_Y P_C and P_Y are all adjacent to allow for minimized movement.

### Right Middle:
OA' column has some SFBs in words like ROAD and WHO'S and SFS in words like ABOUT and DON'T but overall this arrangement provides more positives than it does negatives as a result of being part of the modern vowel block. 

### Right Ring:
UE: column deals with more SFB than OA' however most of the examples consist of downward SFBs like TRUE and BLUE which can be typed in a sliding motion to help increase speed.

### Right Pinky: 
I., is another classic column, but turned into a triangular cluster in order to allow for better rolling motions. This unique position helps while typing things like TO, and YOU, because comma would normally be placed on the bottom row, which would result in a ring/pinky scissor from top row U to bottom row, but now the U and comma keys are placed on the same row allowing for improved ergonomics and naturally angles your hand and wrist in a beneficial way. 


# Recommendations
### Wide-Mod/Thumb Shift:
![wide-mod](https://i.ibb.co/60mgcrwR/415-A3-DBF-F29-D-4146-8771-A5-F0-A1-C057-B9.jpg)
Instead of the typical double pinky shift setup found on most layouts, this modification moves shift and caps-lock to the right thumb since they are similar in function, as well as moving the entire right hand one position over to the right in order to improve the angle in which these new thumb keys are used. This helps lower pinky usage and movement, and you no longer need to decide whether to use left or right shift while typing capital B N or Z keys as there's only one shift key appointed to thumb with no other responsibilities, unlike the pinkies which have their own letters to deal with.

Left thumb spacing is recommended for lowering redirects and increasing rolls, and enter is paired with the left thumb as well because enter and space interact with letters in a very similar way yet never interact with each other meaning no SFB drawbacks. The new backspace position is also much closer, as it is now directly next to the home-row I key.
