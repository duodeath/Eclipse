# Eclipse
## An alternative keyboard layout designed for typing on standard row-staggered keyboards
![eclipse](https://i.ibb.co/zWbbgn6H/A2900669-E6-CC-4-B40-A392-D17-B2-FB7836-A.jpg)


### Summary:

The main objective of Eclipse is to optimize for extremely low SFB percentage, especially on the left ring and pinky fingers because they lack dexterity and anatomical freedom advantages that stronger fingers like middle and index utilize for alt-fingering to achieve even lower SFB. 

ZXQJ are the lowest frequency letters so they are placed in the farthest corners of the keyboard to help reduce movement, stretches, and limits pinky/ring fingers to only 2 main keys rather than full 3 key columns, as well as limiting both indexes to 5 main keys rather than full 6 key index setups. 

The redirects and rolls are both on the low side, which isn't positive or negative. The alternations on the other hand are notably high which isn't necessarily bad either but many typists find rolls to be superior to alternation. The weakest aspect of Eclipse is its high SFS percentage, which is mostly caused by the left middle and left index finger columns, however this is at least slightly mitigated by being on dominant fingers.

## Finger Analysis
![fingermap](https://i.ibb.co/XZfjN8yn/Screenshot-2026-11-12-at-3-19-33-AM.png)
### Left Pinky:
BNZ column is only responsible for roughly 0.01% SFB, 0.10% SFS, and 6.6% usage. This is a popular pinky setup among modern layouts due to its low usage and good SFB/SFS which are great qualities for a pinky column as it is generally the weakest and least dextrous finger.

### Left Ring:
FSX column is similar to BNZ with around 0.01% SFB, 0.16% SFS, and 6.5% usage. This column is also popular as a pinky column found in other layouts, making it a great candidate for the ring finger to handle comfortably.

### Left Middle:
RLH is one of the main sources of SFB and SFS with 0.14% SFB, 0.78% SFS, and 11.8% usage. The letters are arranged in a triangular cluster rather than a straight column which is notable because it promotes lower movement in a small concentrated area as a result of all 3 keys being adjacent to one another. R is a particularly difficult letter to pair with other letters SFB wise, therefore it is placed on a strong and flexible middle finger allowing you to alt-finger almost all SFBs present like RL HR and HL. 

### Left Index:
DTMVKQ is the other main cause of SFS with 0.03% SFB, 1.3% SFS, and 14% usage. The total usage is quite high, but it's mostly contained within the DTM cluster since the outer VKQ keys are responsible for only 1.7%. Most SFBs like DV TD and TM can be alt-fingered or sliding is helpful for bigrams like DM.

### Right Index:
GCWPYJ is a much lighter index with 0.06% SFB, 0.60% SFS, and 8.8% usage. The main SFBs PY CY GY are able to be alt-fingered away due to the central Y key positioning using middle finger for Y and the main SFS sources like W_Y P_C and P_Y are all adjacent to allow for low movement.

### Right Pinky: 
I., is responsible for 0.01% SFB, 0.50% SFS, and 8% usage. This is another classic column, but turned into a triangle cluster in order to allow for better rolling motions. This new position helps while typing things like TO, and YOU, because comma would normally be placed on the bottom row, which would result in a ring/pinky scissor from top row U to bottom row, but now the U and comma keys are placed on the same row. 


# Overview
Eclipse makes some sacrifices to achieve very low SFB, but overall I would say it's worth it. The layout still feels rolly at times because it actually has a high percentage of 3rolls like COULD WOULD WORLD POINT YOU while also maintaining decently low redirects. The ZXQJ positions being assigned where they are is quite nice, blocking off the worst positions of the keyboard and keeping fingers from being overburdened. The fingermap may take some extra time to get used to but I believe it naturally pairs well with the standard row-staggered form factor. I would only stray from the fingermap while alt-fingering away SFBs and some certain SFS like M_V with left index and middle.


# Recommendations
### Wide-Mod/Thumb Shift:
![wide-mod](https://i.ibb.co/60mgcrwR/415-A3-DBF-F29-D-4146-8771-A5-F0-A1-C057-B9.jpg)
Instead of the typical double pinky shift setup found on most layouts, this modification moves shift and caps-lock to the right thumb since they are similar in function, as well as moving the entire right hand one position over to the right in order to improve the angle in which these new thumb keys are used. This helps lower pinky usage and movement, and you no longer need to decide whether to use left or right shift while typing capital B N or Z keys as there's only one shift key appointed to thumb with no other responsibilities, unlike the pinkies which have their own letters to deal with.

Left thumb spacing is recommended for lowering redirects and increasing rolls, and enter is paired with the left thumb as well because enter and space interact with letters in a very similar way yet never interact with each other meaning no SFB drawbacks. The new backspace position is also much closer, as it is now directly next to the home-row I key.
